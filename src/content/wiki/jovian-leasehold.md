Who owns Jupiter’s fuel, and who gets paid to fetch it? The **Jovian Leasehold** is the answer both sides live with. It isn’t a single treaty. It is a set of rules that grew up over generations to manage a standoff. The principle fits in four words: **sovereign title, operational lease.**

- The [United Solar Nations](/wiki/united-solar-nations/) holds **sovereign title**. Every gram of helium-3 in Jupiter’s atmosphere belongs to the USN until it is extracted and sold. Scooping it doesn’t make it yours. You are providing a service to the owner.
- The [Capa](/wiki/capa/) hold **operational leases**. Their crews fly the scoop ships and maintain the arrays in the high-radiation zones. They are paid a performance fee by the tonnage they deliver to the refineries on [Callisto](/wiki/callisto/), not for the fuel itself.

## The Adaptation Gap

Why doesn’t the USN just do the work? Because Jupiter is a radiation machine. Its magnetic field traps particles and accelerates them into belts that kill. Baseline people can live on Callisto almost as safely as on Earth. Everywhere further in, they can’t. Capa crews, adapted and hardened, can work there for a time. The USN owns the mine. The Capa own the hands.

## The map

| Zone | Who runs it | What happens there |
|---|---|---|
| **Callisto** (Green Zone) | USN | Refining, administration, trade |
| **L4 Trojans** (the gatehouse) | USN, with joint stations | Customs, certification, construction |
| **L5 Trojans** (the Wilds) | Capa, in practice | Repair yards, habitats, the shadow economy |
| **Inner moons and atmosphere** (Red Zone) | Capa crews under lease | Scooping, salvage, high-risk work |

## Claims

New helium-3 finds are where the Leasehold gets sharp. A strike is locked first with a **preliminary claim** at a station registry. The full **leasehold filing** can only be made in person at the Jovian Claim Registry on Callisto. The registry fires it to Ceres and to Luna City. The claim becomes irrevocable only when **both** have logged it. That takes forty minutes of light to Ceres and forty-nine to Luna City.


<aside class="science">

## Behind the science

The Leasehold grows out of a gap in real space law. The 1967 Outer Space Treaty forbids any nation from claiming sovereignty over the Moon or other celestial bodies. Yet later national laws, such as the United States’ 2015 Commercial Space Launch Competitiveness Act, let companies own resources they extract.

Owning what you dig but not the ground you dig it from is exactly the kind of split that hardens into doctrine over three centuries. The idea of renting the right to work someone else’s property is ancient; Roman law called it *usufruct*.

The radiation zones are real too. Measured surface doses run from thousands of rem a day on Io to about 0.01 rem a day on Callisto, which is lower than in low Earth orbit. That gradient decides where people can live.

</aside>
