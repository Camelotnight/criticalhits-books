The solar system is big enough that light itself is slow. A radio call or a laser message from [Callisto](/wiki/callisto/) takes **forty minutes** to reach Ceres and **forty-nine** to reach Luna City. A message from [Minerva Station](/wiki/minerva-station/) at L4 takes forty-one minutes to reach Geneva, and forty-three to reach Jupiter. Nobody makes a casual call across the system. You send, and you wait.

That delay is **light lag**. Distant habitats feel it in everything. News is old when it arrives, orders are out of date, and the far end of a conversation can be an hour behind.

## QERN

**QERN** is the exception. It crosses the solar system **instantly**. It is also small and fiercely expensive:

- A message is **512 bytes**, a few short paragraphs.
- It is rationed and priced by the byte. An independent hauler’s daily allowance is a single message.
- A captain pushing an unscheduled QERN burst can spend **240 shu** on it, about a month of fuel for a small ship.
- It is compressed and monitored.

So people save QERN for things that matter: births and deaths, contracts, emergencies, a friend calling in a favor. Big data, like survey files or a registry filing, still goes by laser at the speed of light. Ships and stations keep rows of QERN panels, and the confirmation of a laser filing can come back by QERN long before any light could.

## What it means

Information here has a price and a travel time. Who knows what, and when they knew it, matters more in this world than almost anything else.


<aside class="science">

## Behind the science

The light-lag numbers are real geometry. Jupiter is 5.2 astronomical units from the Sun. The Trojan points sit at the corners of an equilateral triangle, so Minerva is 5.2 AU from Jupiter too: about 778 million kilometers, or 43 minutes of light.

QERN is the book’s one deliberate piece of impossible physics. In the real universe nothing carries information faster than light. Quantum entanglement can’t do it either: the “no-communication theorem” forbids it. So the book keeps its exception tiny and expensive. Instant messages exist, but 512 bytes at a time, metered and watched. That keeps distance, delay and secrecy doing what they do.

</aside>
