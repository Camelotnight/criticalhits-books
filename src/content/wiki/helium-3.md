**Helium-3** is a light, rare form of helium: two protons and one neutron instead of the usual two. Fused with deuterium, a heavy form of hydrogen, it releases enormous energy and very few stray neutrons. That makes for clean fusion: reactors that don’t poison their own walls, and torch drives that don’t cook their crews. By 2325 nearly everything runs on it: Earth’s power grid, every warship, every [torch drive](/wiki/fusion-torch/) and every habitat’s air, heat and spin.

The money is even named after it. See [Shu](/wiki/shu/).

## Where it comes from

- **The Moon.** Helium-3 was first mined from lunar soil, where the solar wind has deposited it for billions of years, at a few parts per *billion*. The USN now bores deep to reach older, richer layers. The easy deposits are gone.
- **Jupiter.** Jupiter’s atmosphere holds helium-3 at tens of parts per *million*, thousands of times richer than lunar soil. It is the largest fuel tank in the solar system, at the bottom of a deep gravity well, inside a radiation belt.

## Scooping

**Scoop ships** dive into Jupiter’s upper atmosphere at tremendous speed. They can’t swallow the whole gas stream; it would melt them. Instead a powerful magnetic field turns most of the flow aside, using it for thrust against the drag. Only a sliver is diverted into a cryogenic plant aboard. A run takes days or weeks, and it yields about a kilogram of helium-3 an hour.

It is brutal work. Hulls ride behind ablative heat shields. Crews ride inside a bubble of magnetic shielding against the radiation. The ship needs enough thrust to climb back out of the well. The crews are almost all [Capa](/wiki/capa/), under lease. See [The Jovian Leasehold](/wiki/jovian-leasehold/).

## Refining

Raw catch isn’t fuel. Separating helium-3 from ordinary helium-4 means cooling the mix below about two kelvin. There, helium-4 turns into a superfluid that slips through filters fine enough to stop helium-3. The plants are huge and must sit on steady ground. They are all on [Callisto](/wiki/callisto/), and the USN owns all of them.

## Behind the science

Jupiter’s helium-3 is real, and we have measured it. On December 7, 1995, NASA’s Galileo probe dropped into Jupiter’s atmosphere. Its mass spectrometer measured the ratio of helium-3 to helium-4 at about 1.66 in 10,000. Combined with how much helium Jupiter holds, that works out to roughly 29 parts per million of helium-3 by mass, vastly richer than the Moon.

Deuterium–helium-3 fusion is a real reaction. It releases most of its energy as charged protons rather than neutrons, which is why it is called nearly “aneutronic.” The catch is that it needs a far hotter plasma to ignite than the deuterium–tritium fuel today’s experimental reactors use. The book assumes that problem was solved, and that whoever held the patents got very rich.
