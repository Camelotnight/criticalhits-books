The main asteroid **Belt** between Mars and Jupiter is the homeland of the [Capa](/wiki/capa/). Its people grew up in light gravity and hard vacuum, and they took that adaptation out to Jupiter.

## The big four

| World | Population | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Ceres** | about 2.8 million | Wet. A quarter of its mass is water ice. Water is nearly free. |
| **Vesta** | about 8.5 million | Dry basalt. Water is imported and rationed like a precious utility. |
| **Pallas** | | Home of the Capa Science Center’s computing array. |
| **Hygiea** | | Wet, like Ceres. |

Water splits the Belt into haves and have-nots. On Ceres and Hygiea it is mined from the ground, and people are relaxed about it. On dry worlds every liter is shipped in, mostly on the long **Callisto Run** from Jupiter, and recycling is a way of life.

## Ceres

Ceres is the Belt’s largest world and the namesake of the Capa alliance, the Ceres Protection Agreement. It is also a registry. A helium-3 leasehold filed on [Callisto](/wiki/callisto/) is sent to the Ceres Administrative Offices, forty light-minutes away, and to Luna City. It isn’t final until both log it. Many Capa ships, the *Cooper’s Reach* among them, carry Ceres registry.

## Older habitats

Not everything in the Belt is new weave. **Hatcher’s Drift**, a deep-Belt habitat, was built on a titanium skeleton, and every joint groaned and rattled. Its deep-bore silicate crushers set a rhythm that people raised there still feel in their chests. Kade Holloway grew up there.

## Why they don’t just spin an asteroid

People ask why Capa don’t hollow out an asteroid and spin it for gravity. Most asteroids are rubble held together by their own weak gravity. Spin one fast enough to feel and it flies apart. So Belt habitats are built *inside* rock for shielding, or in spinning rings and drums anchored *to* it.


<aside class="science">

## Behind the science

The numbers come from real planetary science. Ceres has a density of about 2.16 grams per cubic centimeter and a thick mantle of water ice. Dawn, the NASA spacecraft that orbited it, found bright salt deposits left where briny water reached the surface.

Vesta, by contrast, is a dry, differentiated world of basalt. So the Belt’s economy is built around a real chemical divide: where the water is, and where it isn’t.

</aside>
