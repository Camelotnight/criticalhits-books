Space is big, help is far, and the nearest navy ship can be hours out. Across the Belt and the outer system, rescue is a trade. Independent **search-and-rescue** crews, mostly [Capa](/wiki/capa/), fly cutters built for it, carry a **SAR ticket** (a rescue license) and live by a code.

## The Belt Code

The **Belt Code** is the working law of spacers. It is older and more personal than any statute. Among its rules:

- **First responder.** The first rescue-rated ship to log a claim on an emergency gets the right to the rescue and to any **salvage**. The claim is logged with a timestamp on someone else’s board, so nobody can say later that they got there first.
- **Payment.** Rescue is billed by the hour, plus a hazard premium, from first contact.
- **Fuel.** Every ship keeps an emergency reserve for answering a call.
- **One person, one task.** Two rescuers flying the same line are a collision waiting to happen.

## Salvage law

Salvage law follows the old law of the sea. A salvor doesn’t become the owner of what they save. They earn an **award**, a share of its value, and a **lien** on it until they are paid. The owner of record keeps the property. Only if nobody claims it before the clock runs out does it fall to the salvor.

A taker of someone else’s claim is a **thief**, not a salvor.

## The Golden Handshake

In an emergency, a rescue captain can invoke the **Golden Handshake**. A thumbprint on the console sends out a maritime-priority authorization under the captain’s own SAR ticket. Traffic holds, access locks and construction boards that stay shut to a civilian hull swing open. Everything done under it is logged against the captain’s name and license. It opens every door, and the captain answers for every one.

## Triage

Rescuers sort casualties by color:

- **Red**: needs treatment now.
- **Yellow**: serious, but can wait a little.
- **Black**: dead. *Tag him black* means *leave the dead.*

Crews **locate** a missing crew before they know its status, and **recover** a crew or a boat back aboard. **Bingo** is the time when you must leave, because after it there isn’t enough time or margin to get out.


<aside class="science">

## Behind the science

The Belt Code borrows from real law and real medicine. Maritime salvage really works this way. A salvor is rewarded with a share of the value saved, under contracts like Lloyd’s Open Form and its old principle of *no cure, no pay*. They hold a lien, not ownership.

Color triage is used by emergency services worldwide. The book uses the real terms wherever they exist, on purpose. A reader who knows the trade should trust the page, and a reader who doesn’t should learn it.

</aside>
