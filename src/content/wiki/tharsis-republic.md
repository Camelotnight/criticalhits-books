The **Tharsis Republic** governs Mars. It takes its name from Tharsis, the vast volcanic plateau that holds the solar system’s largest volcanoes. Mars broke from the old inner-system order long ago and has gone its own way since.

## Remaking a planet

Mars is a terraforming project measured in centuries. Two tools carry it.

- **Mirrors.** Huge arrays in orbit focus extra sunlight onto the surface to warm it.
- **Quantum drilling.** Directed-energy rigs, first built to reach Mars’s deep frozen aquifers, bore shafts lined with glass. See [Quantum drilling](/wiki/quantum-drilling/). Those same shafts now tap deep geothermal heat. The Republic runs much of its base-load power on heat from the planet itself instead of on fusion.

That second choice matters politically. Mars used to burn about 85,000 shu of helium-3 a year. It now burns about 20,000. It saves the rest for its navy of roughly 177 ships. That makes the Republic the one power whose lights stay on if Jupiter’s fuel stops flowing. It is also the best customer for helium-3 that slips past the USN’s refineries.

## Mirrors that see

The terraforming mirrors have a second use. Re-angled, an array becomes a telescope kilometers wide, focusing light from distant sectors onto collector satellites. The Republic can see Belt traffic more sharply than anyone on Earth.

## Phobos and Deimos

Mars’s two small moons are its watchtowers and its loading docks.

| | Phobos | Deimos |
|---|---|---|
| Size | 26 × 22 × 18 km | 15 × 12 × 11 km |
| Population | about 85,000 | about 25,000 |
| Role | Industry, defense, trans-shipment | Research and intelligence |

Neither moon is solid rock. Each is a loose pile of gravel and dust held together by its own feeble gravity, 30 to 50 percent empty space. Ordinary mining would make them settle and collapse. Their habitats exist only because quantum drilling melts the rubble as it bores, fusing a glass skeleton into the pile. The rooms aren’t dug out of the moons so much as **manufactured inside them**: pressure vessels fused into gravel.

Both moons sit close enough to the Belt to keep an eye on it with a short light-lag loop. Tethers on Phobos lift cargo between Mars and orbit.

Life on them is cramped and hot. A moon that small has too little surface to radiate away the heat of a big population. Phobos has a second problem: Mars, close below, glows in infrared and warms its radiators. So the Martian moons run on **heat rationing**: low personal energy allowances, strict thermal discipline and sweltering corridors. See [Heat](/wiki/heat/).


<aside class="science">

## Behind the science

Phobos and Deimos really are rubble piles. Phobos has a density of about 1.86 g/cm³ and Deimos about 1.48, far too low for solid rock. They are probably captured asteroids or debris that re-gathered after an ancient impact.

The population ceilings come from heat, not space. Every watt used inside a sealed habitat ends up as heat that must be radiated away, and radiating scales with surface area. Assume about 100 kW of total energy use per person, counting industry and food. Then Phobos’s usable radiating surface tops out near 1.9 million people and Deimos near 700,000. Both moons are nowhere near that today. They are kept small and strategic on purpose.

</aside>
