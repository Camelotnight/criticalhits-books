The **Titan Technate Collective** rules Titan, Saturn’s largest moon, twice as far from the Sun as Jupiter. It is the most self-sufficient power in the solar system, and the least understood.

## The nitrogen supplier

Every breathing atmosphere is mostly nitrogen. Plants need it too, to build protein. And nitrogen is the one thing the outer system is short of. Rocky asteroids hold almost none, and lifting it out of Jupiter’s gravity is ruinously expensive. See [Nitrogen](/wiki/nitrogen/).

Titan has an atmosphere that is 98.4 percent nitrogen, thicker than Earth’s, sitting on a moon with weak gravity. That makes it the cheapest nitrogen in the solar system, even counting the distance. The Technate’s tankers run nitrogen in to the **Hektor depot** among Jupiter’s leading Trojans, and they rarely go anywhere else at Jupiter. The air on [Minerva Station](/wiki/minerva-station/) comes from Titan.

## Running on its own fuel

The Technate barely needs Jupiter’s [helium-3](/wiki/helium-3/). Its reactors run on other fusion fuels: deuterium–lithium hybrids and antimatter-catalyzed designs. It burns about 5,000 shu of helium-3 a year, mostly for research ships. It is the one power that could ride out a total collapse of the Jovian supply chain.

## The augments

Nobody outside Titan has a straight answer about what Technate systems look like under the augments. Their machines don’t work the way anyone else’s do, and they don’t explain them. Spacers have nicknames for that silence: **Null** and **Void**.


<aside class="science">

## Behind the science

Titan’s numbers are real. Its atmosphere is about 98 percent nitrogen at roughly 1.45 times Earth’s sea-level pressure. Surface gravity is only about 0.14 g. Escape velocity is about 2.6 km/s, a fraction of Jupiter’s. A dense atmosphere over a light moon means freighters can use wings and lift to climb, not just rockets.

So the economics flip in a way that surprises people. Titan is nearly twice Jupiter’s distance from the Sun. Yet with fusion drives, distance mostly costs *time*, while gravity costs *fuel*. Hauling nitrogen from Titan beats lifting it out of Jupiter.

</aside>
