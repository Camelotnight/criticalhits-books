Minerva is the United Solar Nations’ anchor at L4, the leading [Trojan](/wiki/the-trojans/) camp. It is a **McKendree cylinder**: a vast tube of [carbon weave](/wiki/carbon-weave/), spun on magnetic-fluid bearings so smooth that you can’t feel it turn. It is a port, a registry, a seat of government and a factory. For a [Capa](/wiki/capa/) captain buried in debt, it is the last place in the system where the math still works.

## Gravity by tier

On a cylinder this size, “gravity” is spin, and it grows the farther you are from the axis. Minerva’s levels are called **tiers**, and they sit kilometers apart. Going toward the skin, from a lighter tier to a heavier one, is going *down*.

| Weight | What is there |
|---|---|
| Zero | The docking axis: the holding stack, the locks, the endcap collars |
| 0.8 gee | Minerva General, the station hospital |
| 0.9 gee | The administrative tier |
| 1.0 gee | Senior offices, one tier down |
| 1.2 gee | **The Sump**: the outer industrial ring at the skin |

Weight here is also class. Offices sit where the gravity is gentle and the air is quiet. The Sump is where the heat and the noise are, and where people born there grow thick-boned to carry 1.2 gee all their lives. Even the word for the ground changes. In the Sump and the working levels it is a **floor** or a **concourse**. In the administrative tiers it is a **terrace** or a **garden**. Never a deck: a deck is something on a ship.

## What it does

- **The spinnery.** In the Sump, Arachne weavers spin carbon from Venus into the nanotube filament that is building Hercules. See [Arachne weavers](/wiki/arachne-weavers/).
- **The registry.** Helium-3 claims are *started* here, with a preliminary lock. They are only finished on [Callisto](/wiki/callisto/).
- **Traffic.** Minerva Traffic clears every ship in the corridor to the Hercules construction site, about 50,000 km away.
- **Government.** The Directorate, the Outer Territories Development Authority, runs the [Leasehold](/wiki/jovian-leasehold/) from here. USN–CPA Joint Administrative Services has handled customs and clearances on the station since 2298.

## Places

- **The promenade**: the commercial strip where the radial elevators land. Beacon glare, coffee stalls, recycler bins bolted to the rail.
- **The Long Quarter**: a Capa treaty zone on its own registry. At its heart is **the pit**, a tiered trading floor under ticker glass where helium-3 contracts, rare-earth futures and whole shifts of spinnery labor trade by hand signal. A sign hangs over it: NO AUTONOMOUS UNITS ON ACTIVE FLOOR.
- **The Sump**: heat, roar and sweet, resin-thick air, with the extruders throbbing through the floor.

## Air, water and coffee

Minerva breathes nitrogen and oxygen shipped from [Titan](/wiki/titan-technate/) through the Hektor depot. Its water runs through a six-stage reclamation system and great cisterns around the skin. See [Water and closed loops](/wiki/water-and-closed-loops/). The hydroponic ring grows real arabica beans, and a promenade stall sells it under the slogan *Real soil. Real beans.* Everyone else drinks [Capa caf](/wiki/daily-life/).

The station keeps three rotating shifts with shift bells, and a slow simulated dawn on the lights. Workers’ coveralls are striped in conductive thread: grey for line crews, amber for supervisors, blue for clearance to skip the queue. Station police are **MinSec**.


<aside class="science">

## Behind the science

A McKendree cylinder is a real concept. NASA engineer Thomas McKendree proposed it in the 1990s as the next step beyond Gerard O’Neill’s steel space colonies. Steel tears itself apart under spin at a radius of a few kilometers. Carbon nanotubes are so strong for their weight that a cylinder could, in theory, reach hundreds of kilometers in radius.

At that size the spin is slow: one turn every several minutes. That is too slow for the inner ear to notice, which is why nobody on Minerva feels it. The only clue that you are inside a spinning drum, not on a planet, is that climbing toward the axis makes you lighter.

</aside>
