**Quantum drilling** is what everyone calls millimeter-wave directed-energy excavation. Despite the name, there is nothing quantum about it. It is a very powerful beam of microwaves.

## How it works

A drill rig has a **gyrotron** at its heart, a microwave source first built for fusion research. It runs at one to five megawatts at frequencies between 30 and 300 gigahertz. The beam travels down a waveguide to the rock face. There it does two things at once:

- **It shatters.** The beam heats water and volatiles trapped in the rock to two or three thousand degrees. They expand violently and burst the rock apart from inside. No grinding, and no drill bit to wear out.
- **It glazes.** The walls of the hole melt and then cool behind the advancing head into a seamless, airtight liner of glass. A quantum-drilled tunnel comes out pre-sealed.

The beam itself is invisible. What you see is the blinding white flash at the focal point, where rock is turning to plasma.

## Why it changed everything

- **Luna.** The Moon’s 450 million people live in the **Warrens**: glass-lined tunnels kilometers deep, shielded from radiation and from attack. The same rigs bore down into deep lunar soil to boil out [helium-3](/wiki/helium-3/).
- **Mars.** The technique was first developed for Martian terraforming, to reach deep aquifers. Now the [Tharsis Republic](/wiki/tharsis-republic/) uses its shafts to tap geothermal heat.
- **Rubble moons.** Phobos and Deimos are loose piles of gravel. Ordinary tunnels would collapse. Quantum drilling fuses the gravel into a glass skeleton as it goes, manufacturing a pressure vessel inside the pile.
- **Venus.** Floating platforms lower a ceramic tether with a waveguide inside to drill the crushing, burning surface from forty kilometers up.

## Its limits

It is ravenous for power, a megawatt or two per rig, all of it fusion. In vacuum there is no air to suck the cuttings out. A shroud seals the drill site, and the vaporized rock shoots up into a cold trap, where it freezes onto baffles as dust.

*Reference note from prior service: helium extraction rig, Luna. Filed for comparison with Belt construction methods.*

**Purpose.** Colleagues at L4 have asked how the inner system builds. This note describes one shaft.

**Method.** A megawatt gyrotron feeds a waveguide. The beam is millimeter-wave and invisible. At the rock face, trapped water and gas flash to vapor and break the rock from inside. The vapor travels up the stem into a sealed shroud. Luna has no air to carry it off, so it is frozen onto chilled plates in a cold trap, and charged plates collect the remainder. The charged plates glow violet while they are working. The crew watches that glow. If it stops, the beam stops.

**Observation.** Behind the drill head the walls melt and set as glass. My first shaft finished as one unbroken liner, black, still warm through a glove. A new liner ticks as it cools. Crews wait for it to go quiet before anyone descends. The wait appears in no manual. I have never seen it skipped.

**Use.** On a helium rig the glass is the pipe that carries gas up to the traps. Deeper in the crust the same glass is housing. The Warrens hold four hundred and fifty million people behind liners made this way. I lived behind one for six years and gave it no thought, which is the best that can be said of a wall.

**Safety.** The beam makes no distinction between rock and a boot. Head count is taken before the gyrotron is keyed and again after. The second count is the one the crew remembers.

**Comparison.** On Luna we melt our housing into the rock. At Hercules they spin it from thread. I was sent to grade the thread. I would like to understand it first.

## Behind the science

This one is real, and close. Millimeter-wave drilling grew out of work by Paul Woskov at MIT’s Plasma Science and Fusion Center. He realized that gyrotrons, built to heat fusion plasmas, could vaporize rock. A company called Quaise Energy is now developing the technique to drill kilometers down for geothermal power ([Quaise’s announcement of a drilling milestone](https://www.quaise.com/news/quaise-energy-achieves-drilling-milestone-with-millimeter-wave-technology)).

Self-glazing walls are part of the real pitch: the beam leaves behind a glassy lining that can seal the borehole. The book takes that idea off Earth, to places where a pre-sealed tunnel is the difference between a hole and a home.
