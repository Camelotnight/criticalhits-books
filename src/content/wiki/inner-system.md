The USN is often taken for a tired empire clinging to Earth. It isn’t. Over three centuries it has rebuilt the inner solar system into one industrial machine, every world assigned a job.

## Earth

About 12.8 billion people, at the bottom of the deepest gravity well in the inner system. Earth runs on helium-3 fusion, which keeps its skies clean and its grid huge. Geneva is the seat of power. Earth makes policy and consumes. It doesn’t export much: lifting anything off it is too expensive.

## Luna

The Moon is the USN’s keystone, home to about **450 million** people. They live in the **Warrens**: tunnels bored kilometers deep with [quantum drilling](/wiki/quantum-drilling/), their walls melted into seamless, airtight glass. The deep crust stays at a steady minus twenty degrees, shields its people completely from radiation, and can’t be bombed from orbit.

Luna was the first source of [helium-3](/wiki/helium-3/). Its soil held only a few parts per billion, deposited by the solar wind over billions of years. The easy deposits are gone, and the deeper ones are going. Every year the USN depends a little more on Jupiter.

Luna City keeps one of the two registries that make a Jovian helium-3 claim final.

## Mercury

Closest to the Sun, Mercury gets up to ten times Earth’s sunlight. The USN’s **Helios Ring** of orbital solar arrays turns that into energy and raw material: power for heavy industry, and particle colliders that make antimatter. Mercury is mostly iron. Its mines feed the USN’s metal, and its people live in crater cities at the poles, where ice survives in permanent shadow.

## Venus

The surface of Venus would crush and cook a lander in hours. Its upper clouds, though, are mild. **Aerostats**, floating refineries, drift there and crack the carbon dioxide atmosphere: carbon for building, oxygen for export. The sulfuric acid clouds give up hydrogen and sulfur.

Venusian carbon is shipped out to Minerva as slurry, labeled *Venusian Sourced — USN Atmospheric Division*. There it becomes the filament that is building [Hercules](/wiki/hercules/).


<aside class="science">

## Behind the science

Venus’s cloud deck is one of the most Earthlike places in the solar system. At around 50 kilometers up, the pressure is about one atmosphere and the temperature is mild. Breathable air would float there, because oxygen and nitrogen are lighter than carbon dioxide.

Turning carbon dioxide into carbon nanotubes isn’t invented either. Laboratories have demonstrated electrochemical processes that split CO₂ into carbon nanotubes and oxygen. An atmosphere that is 96.5 percent CO₂ is the largest carbon mine in the inner system.
