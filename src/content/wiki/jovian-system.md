Jupiter is the largest world in the solar system and the richest. Its atmosphere holds the system’s biggest supply of [helium-3](/wiki/helium-3/), the fusion fuel everything runs on. It also has the most dangerous neighborhood.

## A planet that kills from a distance

Jupiter’s magnetic field is enormous. It traps charged particles from the Sun and from the volcanoes of Io, and it accelerates them into belts of radiation. How close you live to Jupiter decides how long you live.

| Moon | Distance from Jupiter | Surface dose | Status in 2325 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Io** | 421,700 km | about 3,600 rem a day | Total exclusion. Robots only. |
| **Europa** | 671,000 km | about 540 rem a day | Red Zone. Short, shielded sorties. |
| **Ganymede** | 1,070,400 km | about 8 rem a day | Shielded underground habitats only. |
| **Callisto** | 1,882,700 km | about 0.01 rem a day | Green Zone. People live here. |

That gradient is the map of power. Baseline people from the inner system can live on [Callisto](/wiki/callisto/). Inside that, only adapted [Capa](/wiki/capa/) crews work for long, and only for a while. The [Jovian Leasehold](/wiki/jovian-leasehold/) is built on it.

## The gravity well

Jupiter’s gravity makes movement expensive. Going from Callisto in to Europa costs about 8.3 km/s of velocity change, as much as some interplanetary trips. Callisto sits on the rim of the well, so it is the cheapest place to ship refined fuel *out* to the rest of the system.

## The Trojans

Sixty degrees ahead of Jupiter on its orbit, and sixty degrees behind, are two points of balance where the pull of the Sun and the pull of Jupiter hold small bodies steady. Millions of asteroids have gathered there over billions of years: **the Trojans**. The leading camp, **L4**, is the front door from the inner system. [Minerva Station](/wiki/minerva-station/) and the [Hercules](/wiki/hercules/) cylinders are there. The trailing camp, **L5**, is the back door, and it belongs to the Capa in all but name. See [The Trojans](/wiki/the-trojans/).

## Distance

The Trojans aren’t near Jupiter at all. L4, Jupiter and the Sun form an equilateral triangle, so Minerva sits as far from Jupiter as Jupiter sits from the Sun: **5.2 AU**, about 778 million kilometers. Light takes 43 minutes to cross it. See [QERN and light lag](/wiki/qern/).


<aside class="science">

## Behind the science

The radiation figures are close to real estimates of surface dose on the Galilean moons. Io’s dose is lethal in hours. Callisto’s is lower than what astronauts receive in low Earth orbit. Callisto is so far out that it sits mostly beyond Jupiter’s main belts.

The Trojans are real too. Jupiter shares its orbit with thousands of known asteroids, and models suggest hundreds of thousands more. In the real sky the leading L4 swarm is more crowded than the trailing L5 swarm, roughly 1.6 times as many bodies.

The Trojan points really are 60 degrees ahead and behind. They are stable because L4, the Sun and Jupiter form an equilateral triangle.
