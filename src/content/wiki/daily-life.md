Most people in the outer system aren’t captains or diplomats. They are hydroponics techs, spinnery workers, freight handlers and maintenance crews, and their lives are measured in shifts, bills and air.

## Caf

**Capa caf** is the drink of working people: caffeine and protein pressed into one thick, bitter charge, drunk from a sealed **bulb** because a cup would float away. It feeds you and wakes you in the same mouthful. Everything aboard has to earn its mass twice. Nobody claims to like it. The Capa say it is *an acquired taste*, and the joke never gets old.

Real coffee exists. On Minerva the hydroponic ring grows arabica, and a promenade stall sells it: *Real soil. Real beans.* It costs what you would expect.

## Food

Food is grown inside the loop, under lights, from water and air the habitat recycles.

- **Capa nutritional paste**, extruded by shipboard synthesizers from algae and yeast. Standard, filling, joyless.
- **Micro-greens** grown on galley walls for fresh vitamins.
- **Vat-cultured skyr**, a thick, tart, high-protein dairy food made by fermentation and sold at transit kiosks.
- In industrial tiers, a commissary protein base with a vitamin supplement and an electrolyte block for workers sweating out a shift.

Station food tends to be strong-flavored. In low gravity, fluid shifts toward the head, and a stuffy head dulls the sense of taste. See [Food](/wiki/food/) for a Minerva kiosk menu and why everything comes with chili.

## Shifts

Stations run around the clock. Minerva keeps **three rotating shifts**, marked by shift bells, under circadian lighting that brings up a slow simulated dawn. Third shift and first shift share commissary tables. Fatigue is a safety hazard, especially in the hot, loud industrial levels.

## Home

A worker’s quarters in a habitat drum are small. A family shares a room or two, with a child’s drawings on the hatch. A utility ledger by the airlock bills for air, water and heat, and it flashes yellow when the fuel price climbs. The air recyclers thump overhead. When a radiator fails, the drum holds the heat.

## Leaving

People leave for work: a construction queue that pays a hazard premium, a berth on a list. Messages home go by laser and arrive old, or by [QERN](/wiki/qern/), priced so that people spend it on births and deaths. The name of someone who left comes up a little less often each month.


<aside class="science">

## Behind the science

The taste problem is real. Astronauts on the International Space Station often report that food tastes bland. Fluids shift toward the head in microgravity and cause a stuffy, congested feeling, and crews reach for hot sauce.

Precision fermentation, which uses yeast and microbes to make dairy proteins without animals, is real and commercial today. Spirulina and other microalgae are among the most studied foods for closed-loop life support, because they grow fast, recycle carbon dioxide and pack protein.
