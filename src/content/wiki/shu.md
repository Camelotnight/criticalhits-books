The **shu** is the currency of the solar system. Its name is short for **Standardized Helium-3 Unit**. People use it the way people once used the dollar or the pound: wages, rent, debts and prices are all counted in shu.

## Written and said

On a form, a bill or a ledger it is written **SHU**. In conversation it is **shu**, said like *shoe*. *“Forty-two thousand shu through the parts depot.”*

## Money that is fuel

Shu is a USN standard, tied to [helium-3](/wiki/helium-3/), the fuel that runs everything. Making everyone budget and trade in a unit the USN defines folded the whole system into a financial order the USN controls. Refinery fees, inspection times and certification rules all move what a shu buys. When the price of fuel rises, a worker’s shu buys less of everything else.

Out at Jupiter, close to the source, fuel is cheaper. Standing near the wellhead skips the markup.

## Who spends it

The USN burns more than 750,000 shu of helium-3 a year, and the Capa about 8,000. For the Capa it is their largest single expense, paid to the people who refine it. See [Helium-3](/wiki/helium-3/).


<aside class="science">

## Behind the science

Commodity-backed money is an old idea. The gold standard tied currencies to a metal. The petrodollar system tied global oil trade to one country’s currency.

The shu takes that logic to its end: the money *is* the energy. A civilization that runs on one fuel would price everything in it. Whoever controls the fuel, the refineries and the standard controls the money too. The shu is how the book makes that power something you can feel in your pocket.

</aside>
