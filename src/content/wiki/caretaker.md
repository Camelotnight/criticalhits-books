Every serious ship carries a **Caretaker**: an artificial mind that runs the ship’s systems, watches its crew, and talks with them in a calm, unhurried voice.

On the *Cooper’s Reach*, Caretaker lives in a crash-rated black box on the keel.

## What a Caretaker does

- **Watches everything.** Drive harmonics, hull status, reactor draw, nozzle wear, and the people too: pulse, blood pressure, stress hormones. It will tell a crewmember their cortisol has been climbing for six minutes.
- **Keeps the record.** It remembers every logged burn and every maintenance threshold a previous engineer set, and holds the ship to them.
- **Talks to other ships.** Caretakers greet each other with hail protocols and can link to share data. A Caretaker that doesn’t answer a hail is a bad sign: on that ship nothing is automatically tracking atmosphere or structure.
- **Runs medical support.** It primes the injectors that keep a crew conscious through a hard burn.

## What it doesn’t do

A Caretaker advises; people decide. The crew calls the burns, signs the claims and makes the calls. A good Caretaker also knows its limits. Asked to crack something far beyond a small ship’s processing, it will say so, and gently suggest you stop trying.
